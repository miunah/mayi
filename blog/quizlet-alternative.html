Why I Stopped Recommending Quizlet in 2026

Quizlet is still fine if you need to cram thirty vocabulary words the night before a high school quiz, but it is a frustrating tool for serious study in 2026. After watching it lock core review modes behind Quizlet Plus and plaster flashing ads next to flashcards, I realized students were paying thirty-six dollars a year for an app that does not even use real spaced repetition.

Back in early 2025, when I was talking daily with students prepping for medical boards and language exams, everyone had the same complaint. They would hit a paywall after a few rounds of practice, or lose access to their decks on the bus because offline mode was gated behind a subscription.

Even if you pay for Quizlet Plus, its scheduling logic is built for short-term cramming rather than keeping thousands of concepts in your head for a full year. It also forces you to keep a separate app open for your actual lecture summaries.

How We Built Mayinab to Fix This

We built Mayinab as a 100% free, non-profit alternative because study tools should not hold your own notes hostage. Instead of separating your summaries from your flashcards, you write normal notes in a fast chat-style timeline and drop flashcards right inside the same topic using the /flashcard command.

Under the hood, Mayinab runs on the FSRS-5 spaced repetition algorithm rather than basic quiz loops or outdated SM-2 formulas. When I migrated my own Japanese and anatomy decks in early 2026, FSRS-5 cut my daily review load by nearly a third while keeping my recall above ninety percent.

If you already have flashcards elsewhere, you can import CSV or TSV files in seconds, and Mayinab automatically migrates legacy Anki SM-2 history into FSRS-5. You also get native Markdown, instant LaTeX math rendering, a Scribble drawing canvas, and an optional BYOK AI assistant where you can connect your own Gemini, OpenAI, Claude, DeepSeek, Groq, or free local Ollama setup.

Where Quizlet Still Wins (and Who Should Switch)

To be fair, Quizlet still has one big advantage, which is its massive public library of pre-made sets for specific school textbooks. If you just want to search for an existing chapter deck ten minutes before class and play a quick matching game in your browser, Quizlet is convenient.

Your data stays in a local SQLite database with zero ads and no mandatory account, and you can sync across your phone and PC for free via LAN P2P, a monitored cloud folder, or your own Google Drive appDataFolder.
