The Honest State of Flashcard Apps on Mac

If you need a reliable flashcard app on macOS right now, Anki is still the safest daily driver, and Mochi is the cleanest paid pick. I help build Mayinab, and I am not going to pretend our Mac build is already out when it is still on the waitlist.

Back in early 2026, I spent a few weeks testing the usual Mac study stack alongside students who write to us. Anki works great once you turn on FSRS, yet its Qt window still feels awkward next to native macOS apps and its iOS companion costs $24.99.

Quizlet pushes ads and paywalls at every click, RemNote feels heavy when you just want quick cards, and Mochi locks cross-device sync behind a monthly fee. That gap is why Mac users keep asking when Mayinab will arrive.

What Mayinab Does Differently

Mayinab is already live on Windows, Linux, and Android, and the macOS build brings that exact offline-first architecture over. Everything sits in a local SQLite database with FSRS-5 running from day one and zero account setup.

You take notes and create flashcards inside the same chat timeline, sketch diagrams with the Scribble engine, and format formulas in LaTeX without touching card templates. When you want to sync, you pick LAN P2P over Wi-Fi or Tailscale, a monitored folder like Syncthing, Dropbox, OneDrive, or Drive, or your own Google Drive appDataFolder.

We also stay 100% free and non-profit. There is no paid cloud tier, and the optional AI assistant uses your own key or local Ollama models running straight on your machine.
