Why I stopped trusting "free" study apps

Yes, a truly free study app exists, but you have to read the fine print on most of the others. Back in 2025, when I was building my first decks, I kept hitting the same wall.

Quizlet shows ads on the free plan and keeps offline and ad-free study for paying users. Brainscape limits what you can do before you subscribe. StudyBlue is gone, folded into Chegg. Anki is great, but it costs money on iOS.

That is why I build Mayinab the way I do. Every feature is free, it is non-profit, and the app does not ask for an account.

How it fits a normal study day

In class I jot notes in the chat bar, using Markdown when I am in a hurry. When a concept clicks, I type /flashcard and the card lands right under the note.

On the bus I clear my due cards in about ten minutes. At my desk I open split-view, with notes on one side and reviews on the other. Before an exam there is nothing to cram, because the reviews have been happening all along.

Where it runs

Mayinab is out on Android, Windows and Linux. iOS and macOS are not launched yet, but the waitlist is open.

To move between devices you can sync over your local network with a QR code or Tailscale, point it at a watched folder (Syncthing, Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox), or use your own Google Drive. No paid cloud is involved.

It is not perfect, and I would rather say so. If you live inside a huge shared-deck community, Quizlet still has more public decks. For learning on your own schedule, I have not found anything I like more.
