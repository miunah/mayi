Mayinab vs SuperMemo: Which Spaced Repetition App Is Better?

January 10, 2026 · 3 min read

SuperMemo invented spaced repetition software, yet for 99% of students in 2026, Mayinab is the far better tool to actually use every day. When I tested SuperMemo 18 again back in early 2026, I spent more time fighting Windows 95-style menus and window trees than reviewing my cards.

We all owe a huge debt to Dr. Piotr Wozniak for creating the original SM-2 algorithm in 1987. Still, paying $66 for a Windows-only desktop license or dealing with a clunky web subscription makes very little sense now that open algorithms have caught up.

FSRS-5 vs SM-18 in Real Life

SuperMemo fans love pointing out that SM-18 left the old 1987 SM-2 formula behind decades ago. They are right about SM-2 feeling dated, which is exactly why we built Mayinab around FSRS-5 instead of sticking to legacy intervals.

When I migrated my old Anki CSV decks into Mayinab in late 2025, FSRS-5 converted the legacy SM-2 history automatically and cut my daily review load by roughly 25 percent. Unless you maintain a twenty-year SuperMemo collection with tens of thousands of items, you will not notice any scheduling advantage in SM-18 over FSRS-5.

The Interface Gap and Incremental Reading

The one place where SuperMemo still stands alone is incremental reading. If your favorite workflow is pasting fifty-page academic articles into a knowledge tree and chopping them into cloze deletions over six months, SuperMemo is built specifically for your brain.

Most students I talk to every week hate studying like that. They want to open their laptop or phone, jot down lecture notes, sketch a quick diagram on the Scribble canvas, and type /flashcard right inside the same timeline without taking a three-hour tutorial first.

Mayinab also gives you optional BYOK AI support if you want to plug in your own Gemini, OpenAI, Claude, DeepSeek, Groq, OpenRouter, or free local Ollama key to speed up card drafting. Everything stays in a local SQLite database on your machine with zero ads and no account required.

Platforms, Sync, and What to Pick

Because I switch between a Linux desktop and an Android phone all day, SuperMemo is a non-starter for my routine. With Mayinab, I sync across Windows, Linux, and Android for free using LAN P2P via QR code or Tailscale, a monitored Syncthing folder, or my own Google Drive appDataFolder.

We also have an open waitlist for iOS and macOS right now while we finish polishing those builds. Stick with SuperMemo if you live on Windows and worship incremental reading, or grab Mayinab if you want modern FSRS-5 scheduling without the 1990s friction.
