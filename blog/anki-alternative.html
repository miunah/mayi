Where Anki wears people down

Anki is legendary for customization, yet that same flexibility turns basic setup into homework. New users hit a wall of note types, card templates, CSS fields, and sync quirks before they even review ten cards.

Worse, Anki only handles flashcards. You still end up taking class notes in Notion, OneNote, or Google Docs, then copy-pasting quotes back and forth until you fall behind and quit both tools.

What we built differently in Mayinab

In Mayinab, every subject opens into topics with a simple chat-style timeline. You drop a Markdown note, a LaTeX equation, a PDF, a Scribble sketch, and a flashcard in the exact same thread, so your cards always keep their context.

Under the hood, everything lives in a local SQLite database with FSRS-5 spaced repetition turned on by default. You do not create an account, you never see an ad, and you never hit a paywall because the project is 100% free and non-profit.

By May 2026, we had also shipped three free ways to sync without running a paid cloud server. You can sync over LAN P2P using a QR Code or Tailscale, point Mayinab at a monitored folder like Syncthing, Dropbox, or OneDrive, or connect your own Google Drive appDataFolder.

If you want AI help turning messy notes into draft flashcards, you can plug in your own API key from OpenAI, Gemini, Claude, DeepSeek, Groq, or OpenRouter, or run Ollama locally for free. If you hate AI in study tools, leave it turned off and the app stays completely quiet.

Who should stay on Anki

Be honest about your workflow before switching. If you are on an iPhone or Mac right now and cannot wait for our iOS and macOS releases, or if your med-school deck depends on five specific Anki add-ons, staying on Anki makes total sense.
