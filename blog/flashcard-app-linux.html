Anki vs Mayinab on Linux in 2026

If you study on Linux in 2026, Anki and Mayinab are the only two offline-first spaced repetition apps worth installing. Anki still wins when you depend on massive AnkiWeb shared decks, while Mayinab is faster to live in if you want your notes, FSRS-5 flashcards, and sketches in one place.

Back in 2025, I got tired of running a three-app circus on Fedora. I took lecture and docs notes in Obsidian, copy-pasted definitions into Anki, and fought with Qt scaling every time I moved windows between monitors.

When we built Mayinab, the goal was simple, keep everything inside a local SQLite database and stop forcing students to glue note apps and flashcard apps together.

Why FSRS-5 and Local Sync Matter on Linux

Anki makes you dig through deck options to turn on FSRS, and most newer web apps lock you into a monthly cloud plan. Mayinab runs FSRS-5 out of the box and stays 100% free and non-profit.

In early 2026, I tested syncing my Linux laptop with my Android phone over Tailscale using Mayinab's LAN P2P mode, and later through a Syncthing folder. Both worked with zero accounts and zero cloud fees, and you can also use your own Google Drive appDataFolder if you prefer.

On desktop, you get resizable split-view, a Ctrl+K command palette, full Markdown and LaTeX, a Scribble drawing engine for diagrams, and optional BYOK AI that talks straight to local Ollama if you never want study notes leaving localhost.

When You Should Stick With Anki

I am not going to tell every Linux user to drop Anki. If your routine revolves around pre-made AnkiWeb decks for med school or language immersion, or if you rely on ten custom Python add-ons, staying on Anki makes total sense.

You should also stick with Anki for now if you sync with an iPhone or Mac every day. Mayinab runs today on Linux, Windows, and Android, while our macOS and iOS builds are still on the waitlist.
